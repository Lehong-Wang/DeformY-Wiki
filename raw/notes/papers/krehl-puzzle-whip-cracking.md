# The Puzzle of Whip Cracking — Krehl, Engemann, Schwenkel

**One-line gist**: 9 kHz shadowgraph high-speed photography of a whip cracker correlated with shock-wave emission; shows the sonic boom is emitted at the *turn-around* (M ≈ 2), not at M = 1.

**Task setup**: Experimental high-speed photography of a whip crack. Pre-trigger framing 9 kHz camera (256×128 CCD), triggered by acoustical sensor and synchronized with a pulsed copper-vapour laser; direct shadowgraph visualization.

**Sim vs real**: Real experimental measurement.

**Learning method**: None — experimental physics.

**Action representation**: N/A.

**Why cited in the surveys**: Cited as the foundational empirical physics reference. Reports peak tip Mach 2.19 (~750 m/s), peak tip acceleration ~50,000 g, supersonic phase ~1.2 ms. The "turn-around-emits-the-shock" finding empirically justifies why a *preparatory backward swing* (Edraki 2024) couples so tightly to tip energy: the loop accelerates only after the tip has been pre-positioned for a turn-around. Sets the *achievable* tip-velocity envelope (~750 m/s, 50 000 g) — wildly beyond any sub-sonic robot whipping task.

**Key result (if any)**: Peak tip Mach 2.19; peak tip acceleration ~50,000 g; supersonic phase ~1.2 ms; sonic boom at turn-around (M ≈ 2), not at M = 1.
