# Goal-Conditioned Dynamic Manipulation of Ropes / Deformable Linear Objects: A Structured Literature Survey

## TL;DR
- The literature directly on **rope-tip targeting via a learned policy** is small but well-defined: it is anchored by Chi et al.'s **Iterative Residual Policy (IRP, RSS 2022 / IJRR 2024)** for whipping a rope to a 3D point, and is being extended in 2024–2026 by **Bai et al. (SIMPAR 2025)** with deep-RL whip targeting in MuJoCo, **Lan et al. (DIDP, arXiv 2505.17434, 2025)** with diffusion policies for 3D rope whipping, **Wiggle and Go!** for zero-shot system-ID-then-act, and **Wang & Qureshi (IPA, arXiv 2502.05696, 2025)** for soft-tool casting of rigid payloads. Real2Sim2Real (Lim et al., ICRA 2022) is the planar analog.
- For methodological transfer, the most influential analogs are **TossingBot (Zeng et al., RSS 2019/T-RO 2020)** with residual physics on top of ballistic priors, **FlingBot (Ha & Song, CoRL 2021)** and **DextAIRity (Xu et al., RSS 2022)** for dynamic deformable manipulation, plus a flourishing 2024–2025 line on **goal-conditioned throwing with motion manifold primitives (DA-MMP, DMMP)** and whole-body throwing (Hutter group).
- The simulation toolbox for sim-to-real rope policies is now mature: **Discrete Elastic Rods (Bergou et al., SIGGRAPH 2008)** with derivatives by **Jawed et al.** and the new **DEFORM** (differentiable DER, 2024) and **DER-MuJoCo** (Chen et al., 2023/2025) integrations; **PyElastica/Elastica (Naughton et al., RA-L 2021)** Cosserat rods; **Position-Based Dynamics / XPBD** (Liu et al., 2022); plus generic deformable support in **MuJoCo 3 flex**, **Isaac Lab/PhysX/Newton**, **DiffTaichi**, and **Nimble**. The classical physics of bullwhips (Goriely & McMillen, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 2002) shows the crack is generated by the rolling loop reaching supersonic speed, which constrains what any policy can/should produce.

---

## Key Findings

1. **There is exactly one canonical formulation** of "learn a robot policy to whip a rope tip to a 3D target point": IRP (Chi et al., 2022). All subsequent work either (a) iteratively refines on top of IRP's iterative-residual idea (Wiggle and Go!, 2024; Prior-Reinforce/ADAP 2025), (b) replaces the residual idea with system-identification + open-loop control (IPA 2025; DIDP 2025; Bai et al. SIMPAR 2025), or (c) reduces the task to 2D planar casting (Real2Sim2Real, ICRA 2022).
2. **Action representations cluster into three families**: (i) low-dimensional trajectory parameters / via-points / DMPs (IRP, IPA, Real2Sim2Real, Robots-of-the-Lost-Arc, Bai 2025, DA-MMP); (ii) full joint trajectories from RL (Lan et al. DIDP, Wang & Xiong BioRob 2024, Hutter group throwing); (iii) pick-stretch-fling primitives borrowed from cloth (FlingBot).
3. **Sim-to-real is dominated by simulation-side parameter ID** (real2sim2real, GenORM, DEFORM, DiffCloud) followed by deterministic open-loop or iterative residual roll-outs in real. Pure end-to-end RL policies trained in sim and zero-shot deployed are rare for ropes; most successful real-world systems include online residual correction or per-rope ID.
4. **The Yamakawa/Senoo/Ishikawa (Univ. Tokyo) line** is the foundational classical work on dynamic high-speed rope/ribbon/cloth manipulation. It is *not* learning-based — it relies on simplified algebraic deformation models valid only at high speed, plus 1 kHz vision — but it established the tasks (knotting, ribbon shaping, casting) that learning-based methods now revisit.
5. **The bullwhip-physics literature (Goriely & McMillen)** is largely orthogonal but important: it tells you what the rope is *capable* of doing (a loop reaching Mach 1 produces the crack; tip can reach ~Mach 2). This sets a physical ceiling on what any rope simulator must reproduce to be useful for whip policies, and explains why Cosserat / DER models with implicit integration are the right choice.

## Details

### 1. Direct matches: rope/DLO + dynamic motion + learned goal-conditioned policy targeting a 3D point

**Iterative Residual Policy (IRP) — Chi, Burchfiel, Cousineau, Feng, Song. RSS 2022; extended in IJRR 2024 (vol. 43).** *"Iterative Residual Policy: For Goal-Conditioned Dynamic Manipulation of Deformable Objects"* (arXiv 2203.00663).
- **Task setup:** UR5 arm grips one end of a rope (≈100 cm); task is to whip the *free tip* through a green-cross 3D target. Cloth-swinging is a sister task in the same paper.
- **Sim vs. real:** Trained only in simulation (PyBullet rope), executed on real UR5; generalizes to multiple ropes with unknown parameters and to a different robot embodiment. The authors explicitly note non-trivial sim-to-real gap that the iterative residual structure is designed to absorb.
- **Learning method:** Implicit policy via a **delta-dynamics network** that predicts how a small perturbation to a previously executed trajectory changes the resulting tip trajectory; combined with adaptive action sampling and visual feedback to converge in a handful of real trials. Not RL in the conventional sense — closer to learned iterative learning control.
- **Action representation:** Parameterized arm swing trajectory (a small set of waypoint/profile parameters per swing), iteratively perturbed.

**Dynamic Manipulation of Deformable Objects in 3D: Simulation, Benchmark and Learning Strategy — Lan et al. arXiv 2505.17434, May 2025 ("DIDP, Dynamics Informed Diffusion Policy").**
- **Task setup:** Rope whipping with a two-joint robotic arm; target is a 3D goal location in air for the rope tip. Authors explicitly position this as a 3D extension beyond IRP-style tasks.
- **Sim vs. real:** Builds a new reduced-order simulation framework and benchmark; emphasis is on policy learning in their sim with physics-informed test-time adaptation (no extensive real-world results highlighted).
- **Learning method:** Diffusion policy that learns inverse dynamics in a reduced-order space; imitation pretraining + physics-informed test-time adaptation (kinematic boundary conditions, structured dynamics priors imposed during diffusion sampling).
- **Action representation:** Sequence of joint configurations Q ∈ ℝ^{N×D} (full joint trajectory) generated by the diffusion policy.

**Deep Reinforcement Learning of Robotic Manipulation for Whip Targeting — Bai, Wang, Xiong, Boukas. IEEE SIMPAR 2025.**
- **Task setup:** 7-DOF robot arm holding a multi-segment whip in MuJoCo, must hit a 3D target in <1.5 s; whip nodes modeled as 2-DOF rotational spring-damper systems.
- **Sim vs. real:** Pure simulation in MuJoCo; an earlier learning-based control framework with sim2real and OIAC (Online Impedance Adaptation Control) is described for hardware in a related Wang & Xiong 2024 BioRob paper ("A Learning-Based Control Framework for Human-Like Whip Targeting").
- **Learning method:** Compares Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) against PPO, TD3, nonlinear optimization, and a genetic algorithm; SAC outperforms others in generalization across target locations.
- **Action representation:** Joint-space actions with the state being (Δx, Δy, Δz) between whip tip and target.

**A Learning-Based Control Framework for Human-Like Whip Targeting — Wang & Xiong. IEEE BioRob 2024.**
- **Task setup:** Fast (<1.5 s) accurate whip targeting; pipeline = motion planner (learned/optimized) + Online Impedance Adaptation Control (OIAC) + sim2real + visual feedback.
- **Sim vs. real:** Combination — uses MuJoCo to plan, OIAC for compliance on real hardware, visual feedback for closed-loop correction.
- **Learning method:** Learned/optimized motion planner with adaptive impedance; explicitly inspired by Sternad/Hogan dynamic-primitives perspective on human whip control.
- **Action representation:** Parameterized arm motions (compatible with dynamic-primitive framing).

**Wiggle and Go! System Identification for Zero-Shot Dynamic Rope Manipulation — referenced in IJRR 2023/24 as follow-up to IRP.**
- **Task setup:** Goal-conditioned dynamic rope manipulation, but explicitly *zero-shot at task time* (no per-target iteration on real hardware). Rope held at one end, dynamic motion to drive the rope to a goal configuration.
- **Sim vs. real:** Two-stage real-world framework: (1) execute a short repeatable "wiggle" probe to identify physical parameters from observed rope motion; (2) feed parameters into an optimization-based goal-conditioned action predictor and execute zero-shot.
- **Learning method:** Learned simulation prior (system-ID network) followed by optimization-based action selection; the wiggle stage is the learned prior, the action stage is optimization.
- **Action representation:** Parameterized open-loop swing actions; the system-ID conditions a goal-conditioned optimizer rather than a deep policy.

**Implicit Physics-aware Policy (IPA) for Dynamic Manipulation of Rigid Objects via Soft Body Tools — Wang & Qureshi. arXiv 2502.05696, 2025.**
- **Task setup:** A heterogeneous rope-plus-payload system: a rope is attached to a rigid object and the robot dynamically casts the rope so that the rigid payload reaches a *target 3D location* (e.g., for search-and-rescue/alpine casting). Tip-conditioned in spirit, but the conditioning is on the payload landing, not the tip itself.
- **Sim vs. real:** Sim-to-real; explicitly designed for *one-shot* real execution, contrasting with iterative IRP and Real2Sim2Real.
- **Learning method:** Implicit physics-aware policy that performs system identification implicitly and predicts a goal-conditioned, one-shot action.
- **Action representation:** Single high-speed casting motion parameterized as a low-dimensional action vector.

**Real2Sim2Real: Self-Supervised Learning for Planar Robot Casting — Lim, Huang, Chen, Wang, Ichnowski, Seita, Laskey, Goldberg. ICRA 2022 (arXiv 2111.04814).**
- **Task setup:** UR5 holds one end of a cable; one *planar* (table-plane) motion sends the other end to a desired 2D target on the plane, possibly outside the robot's reach. *Not 3D tip-targeting in the air, but the closest 2D goal-conditioned dynamic casting work.*
- **Sim vs. real:** Real2Sim2Real pipeline — collect physical trajectories, tune simulator parameters via Differential Evolution, then generate sim data and learn a policy with weighted sim+real data.
- **Learning method:** Supervised regression (Gaussian Process or MLP) from target → action; simulators evaluated: Isaac Gym (segmented & hybrid) and PyBullet.
- **Action representation:** Parameters of a single planar wrist motion (action ∈ ℝ^k for some small k).

**Robots of the Lost Arc — Zhang, Ichnowski, Seita, Wang, Huang, Goldberg. ICRA 2022 (arXiv 2011.04840).**
- **Task setup:** Fixed-endpoint cable with the other end held by a UR5; tasks include vaulting cables over obstacles, knocking objects from pedestals, and weaving — i.e., target objects/locations in 3D. Learns a 3D apex point that, with a task-specific minimum-jerk trajectory, produces an arcing dynamic motion.
- **Sim vs. real:** Self-supervised data collection on the real UR5 with simulated initialization; physical experiments on 5 cables.
- **Learning method:** Self-supervised deep imitation learning (regression to apex point given target).
- **Action representation:** A single 3D **apex point** that parameterizes a quadratic-program-derived minimum-jerk trajectory through joint limits.

**Learning Deformable Object Manipulation Using Task-Level Iterative Learning Control ("Flying Knots") — recent arXiv 2025/2026.**
- **Task setup:** A robot ties a "flying knot" by a single dynamic upward/twist motion that flips the rope tip into a self-formed loop. Goal is a topological state, not a 3D point, but is solved via dynamic tip-trajectory targeting.
- **Sim vs. real:** Real-world iterative learning; reports 100% success within 10 trials across 7 ropes (including chains and surgical tubing) and ~2–5 trials of transfer between ropes.
- **Learning method:** Task-level iterative learning control (ILC), conceptually descended from IRP.
- **Action representation:** Parameterized one-shot dynamic motion.

**Self-Supervised Physics-Informed Manipulation of DLOs (SPiD) — Long, Solak, Zeynalpour, Zhang, Ajoudani. arXiv 2602.03623, 2025.**
- **Task setup:** Dynamic stabilization/shape control of ropes (rope-stabilization task shown), not pure tip-targeting, but uses dynamic-regime motions.
- **Sim vs. real:** Differentiable mass-spring sim with system ID, neural controller trained via self-supervision in differentiable physics, deployed real.
- **Learning method:** Self-supervised training of a neural controller via task-oriented cost back-propagated through a differentiable mass-spring rope model.
- **Action representation:** Direct neural controller output (joint or end-effector velocity commands).

### 2. Dynamic DLO manipulation **without** target conditioning (foundational and methodologically related)

**Yamakawa, Namiki, Ishikawa (Univ. Tokyo "high-speed flexible robotics" line, 2007–2017).** Multiple ICRA/IROS/IJARS papers.
- *"Motion Planning for Dynamic Knotting of a Flexible Rope with a High-Speed Robot Arm"* (IROS 2010); *"Simple Model and Deformation Control of a Flexible Rope using Constant, High-Speed Motion of a Robot Arm"* (ICRA 2012); *"Dynamic High-Speed Knotting of a Rope by a Manipulator"* (IJARS 2013); *"Dexterous Manipulation of a Rhythmic Gymnastics Ribbon..."* (ICRA 2013); chapter "Toward Dynamic Manipulation of Flexible Objects by High-Speed Robot System: From Static to Dynamic" (IntechOpen 2019).
- **Task setup:** 4-DOF Barrett WAM at very high speed plus 1 kHz vision and tactile; tasks include high-speed knot tying, ribbon-shape generation, dynamic cloth folding. Dynamic regime where rope behavior can be approximated algebraically given high-speed robot motion. Goal is task-success, not 3D tip-position.
- **Sim vs. real:** Real-world only; planning in a simplified deformation model derived analytically.
- **Learning method:** None — model-based motion planning + visual servoing; included here because it is *the* canonical classical reference for dynamic rope manipulation.
- **Action representation:** Pre-planned, model-derived joint trajectories executed at 1 kHz with high-speed visual feedback corrections.

**Zimmermann, Poranne, Coros — "Dynamic Manipulation of Deformable Objects with Implicit Integration."** *IEEE RA-L* + ICRA 2021.
- **Task setup:** Robot arm grasps one end of a deformable cable/cloth and produces dynamic motions to achieve desired shapes. Goal is a configuration, not specifically a 3D tip point.
- **Sim vs. real:** Mostly simulation; analytic derivatives through implicit integration enable model-based control; real-world demos.
- **Learning method:** Model-based optimal control — Newton/DDP through an implicit-integration deformable simulator with analytic gradients.
- **Action representation:** Open-loop end-effector trajectory optimized via DDP / batch Newton.

**Accurate Simulation and Parameter ID of DLOs using DER in Generalized Coordinates / DER-MuJoCo — Chen, Bretl, Vuong, Pham. arXiv 2310.00911 (extended for IROS 2025).**
- **Task setup:** Robot arm flings a wire precisely between two obstacles (dynamic flinging task; goal = pass-through) — a near-direct match for "dynamic motion of rope toward a spatial target" but framed as obstacle clearance, not tip-targeting.
- **Sim vs. real:** Pure-sim training (MuJoCo + DER), zero-shot deployment to a real robot at 76.7% success.
- **Learning method:** Policy optimization (RL-style) on DER-MuJoCo simulator data.
- **Action representation:** Open-loop joint-trajectory parameters.

**Differentiable DER for Real-Time Modeling of DLOs (DEFORM) — arXiv 2406.05931, 2024.** Differentiable DER + neural residual; tested on real two-arm setup. Methodologically central enabling tool for dynamic rope policies.

**Dynamic Flex-and-Flip Manipulation of DLOs — arXiv 2304.00442.** Dynamic two-finger flipping for grasp acquisition; quasi-dynamic, but uses inertial effects.

**HULK / LOKI / SPiDERMan / SGTM / SGTM 2.0 (Berkeley AUTOLAB, Goldberg group).** RSS 2021, etc.
- **Task setup:** Untangling dense knots in cables up to 3 m on a da Vinci or bimanual setup. *Quasi-static* dominantly, but uses learned manipulation primitives that operate on highly nonlinear cable configurations; included because they are the seminal cable-manipulation policies adjacent to dynamic methods.
- **Sim vs. real:** Sim-to-real (Blender cable simulator + real da Vinci).
- **Learning method:** Learned keypoint detection + geometric planner (HULK), learned manipulation features + recovery policies.
- **Action representation:** Pick-and-place manipulation primitives.

**Combining Self-Supervised Learning and Imitation for Vision-Based Rope Manipulation — Nair et al., ICRA 2017.** Inverse-dynamics CNN with 60K self-supervised interactions; quasi-static rope shaping into target image. Foundational pre-cursor for image-conditioned rope manipulation.

**Wu, Yan, Kurutach, Pinto, Abbeel — "Learning to Manipulate Deformable Objects without Demonstrations." RSS 2020.** Iterative pick-place action space for deformables; quasi-static.

**Sundaresan et al., Grannen et al.** A long line of Berkeley papers (CoRL/ICRA/RSS 2020–2022) on cable descriptors, dense object descriptors trained on synthetic depth, autonomous untangling — quasi-static but seminal for cable representations.

**In-air Knotting of Rope using Dual-Arm Robot based on Deep Learning — Kudoh et al., IROS 2021.** Deep-learning policy for dual-arm in-air knotting; sensorimotor learning, not goal-conditioned to 3D.

**Cable manipulation with a tactile-reactive gripper — She, Wang, Dong, Sunil, Rodriguez, Adelson. *Sci. Robot.* 2020.** Closed-loop tactile control for cable following; quasi-static but landmark.

**GenORM: Generalizable One-shot Rope Manipulation with Parameter-Aware Policy — arXiv 2306.09872, CoRL/ICRA 2024.** Parameter-aware policy + differentiable physics ID from one real demo; quasi-static.

### 3. Goal-conditioned throwing/hitting of rigid objects (methodological analogs)

**TossingBot — Zeng, Song, Lee, Rodriguez, Funkhouser. RSS 2019 (Best Systems Paper); T-RO 2020.**
- **Task setup:** Robot picks arbitrary objects from a bin and throws them into target boxes outside its kinematic reach.
- **Sim vs. real:** Real-world self-supervised + simulator (PyBullet) for ablation; 500+ picks/hour, 85% throw accuracy.
- **Learning method:** End-to-end joint training of grasping + throwing with **residual physics** — a CNN predicts a residual δ on top of a ballistic-physics estimate of release velocity.
- **Action representation:** Release velocity v_{x,y} (magnitude) at a fixed release position with 45° angle; pixel-wise grasp predictions.

**Kober, Mülling, Kroemer, Lampert, Schölkopf, Peters. "Movement templates for learning of hitting and batting" (ICRA 2010 / IROS 2010).** DMP-based learning to hit a ball at a varying target with required velocity at a specific time (table tennis / baseball forehand).

**Ghadirzadeh, Maki, Kragic, Björkman.** Several works on RL with shared synergies / DMPs for throwing. Foundational reference cited by TossingBot.

**MC-PILOT — Data-efficient Robotic Object Throwing with Model-Based RL. arXiv 2502.05595, 2025.** Probabilistic model learned from real data, policy optimized for throwing arbitrary rigid objects to user-specified targets on a Franka Panda; also handles release-error distribution.

**DA-MMP — Dynamics-Aware Motion Manifold Primitives. Chu & Xu, arXiv 2509.23721, 2025.**
- **Task setup:** Goal-conditioned ring-tossing on a real 7-DoF arm.
- **Sim vs. real:** Mostly real-world; sim used for trajectory collection.
- **Learning method:** Learns a low-dimensional manifold of feasible throwing trajectories (variable length); a conditional flow-matching model in latent space, fine-tuned with a small real dataset to bridge the dynamics gap.
- **Action representation:** Variable-length parametric trajectories on a learned latent manifold.

**Differentiable Motion Manifold Primitives (DMMP) — arXiv 2410.12193, 2024.** Same flavor: 7-DoF dynamic ball-throwing to arbitrary 3D target points; encodes a continuous-time differentiable manifold of trajectories.

**Whole-Body Dynamic Throwing with Legged Manipulators — arXiv 2410.05681, 2024.** Sim-trained RL policy for throwing toward arbitrary 3D targets, deployed on humanoid; emphasizes whole-body coordination.

**Dynamic Throwing with Robotic Material Handling Machines — ETH Hutter group, arXiv 2405.19001, 2024.** RL for hydraulic excavator-style underactuated arm to throw to targets; sim-to-real.

**Learning Accurate Whole-body Throwing with High-frequency Residual Policy and Pullback Tube Acceleration — arXiv 2506.16986, 2025.** Whole-body EE-velocity tracking + high-frequency residual policy + corrective acceleration optimizer; nominal RL policy.

**Throwing Objects into a Moving Basket While Avoiding Obstacles — arXiv 2210.00609.** RL in Gazebo, sim2real, throws rigid objects to dynamic targets.

**Dynamic Handover: Throw and Catch with Bimanual Hands — arXiv 2309.05655.** Multi-agent RL in sim, sim2real.

**Catch It! / SpikePingpong / RAI Institute robot baseball / Humanoid Goalkeeper / Humanoid Whole-Body Badminton (arXiv 2511.11218).** Recent striking/catching analogs that use RL or imitation with motion priors and PPO in Isaac Gym; useful for action-representation comparisons.

**Yamakawa, Senoo, Ishikawa et al. — high-speed batting robot.** 1 kHz vision + high-speed arm hit a ball with near-perfect strike-zone accuracy; classical reference for non-learning dynamic hitting.

### 4. Classical whip / bullwhip control and simulators

**Goriely & McMillen — "Shape of a Cracking Whip." *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 88, 244301 (2002).** Foundational physics paper. A simple dynamical model of a tapered, tensioned elastic rod; predicts that the supersonic shock comes from a propagating *loop* (not just the tip), with the loop reaching Mach 1 while the tip eventually reaches ~Mach 2. This is the physics any rope simulator used for whip policies must reproduce.

**Goriely et al., Krehl et al.** Earlier high-speed-photography/sonic-boom literature on bullwhip cracks (1905, 1927 photographs, multiple PRL/Phys. Rev. follow-ups).

**McMillen & Goriely. "Whip Waves." *Physica D* 184, 192–225 (2003).** Wave-propagation analysis of whips.

**Krotov, Russo, Nah, Hogan, Sternad — "Motor control beyond reach—how humans hit a target with a whip." *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 9: 220581 (2022).** Behavioral / motor-neuroscience study of humans hitting a 3D target with a 1.6 m bullwhip; reports strategies in both rhythmic and discrete styles. Companion paper: **Nah, Krotov, Russo, Sternad, Hogan — "Learning to manipulate a whip with simple primitive actions — A simulation study." *iScience* 26, 107395 (2023)** uses simulated dynamic primitives. **Nah et al. (BioRob 2020) — "Dynamic Primitives Facilitate Manipulating a Whip."** **Edraki, Lokesh, Krotov, Ramezani, Sternad (BioRob 2024) — "Human-Inspired Control of a Whip: Preparatory Movements Improve Hitting a Target."** **Xiong, Nah, Krotov, Sternad — "Online Impedance Adaptation Facilitates Manipulating a Whip."** This Sternad/Hogan/Northeastern–MIT cluster is the human-motor-control parallel to the robotics whip-targeting literature and is the source that the Bai/Wang/Xiong robotics group builds on.

#### Cosserat-rod simulators
**Elastica / PyElastica — Naughton, Sun, Tekinalp, et al. *IEEE RA-L* 2021** (Gazzola Lab, UIUC). Open-source Python implementation of Cosserat rod theory; widely used for soft robots, octopus-arm control, magnetic soft robots, MARL experiments. Direct usability for rope-whipping policies if extended with a rigid robot arm.

**SoMo (Soft Motion) — IROS 2021/HRI.** Pybullet-based continuum-manipulator simulator; SoMoGym extends with RL benchmark environments.

**SoMA — A Real-to-Sim Neural Simulator for Robotic Soft-body Manipulation. arXiv 2602.02402, 2026.** Gaussian-splat-based neural simulator; not classical Cosserat but a 2026 entrant.

#### Discrete elastic rods (DER) and derivatives
**Bergou, Wardetzky, Robinson, Audoly, Grinspun — "Discrete Elastic Rods." ACM SIGGRAPH 2008.** The original DER formulation. **Bergou, Audoly, Vouga, Wardetzky, Grinspun — "Discrete Viscous Threads." SIGGRAPH 2010.**

**Jawed, Novelia, O'Reilly — *A Primer on the Kinematics of Discrete Elastic Rods* (Springer, 2018).** Standard reference. Jawed group has produced many follow-ups: DER for knots, soft-robot locomotion, and **Tong, Choi, Qin, Huang, Joo, Jawed — "Sim2real neural controllers for physics-based robotic deployment of deformable linear objects." *IJRR* 43:6 (2024).** **MAT-DiSMech (Lahoti & Jawed, 2026)** — MATLAB DDG-based simulator for rods/shells.

**Choi, Tong, Jawed, Joo — "Implicit Contact Model for DER in Knot Tying." *J. Appl. Mech.* (2021).**

**DEFORM (Differentiable DER for Real-Time DLO Modeling) — arXiv 2406.05931, 2024.** Differentiable physics + neural residual; combined with ARMOUR receding-horizon planner; outperforms learned dynamics baselines on real DLOs.

**On Planar Discrete Elastic Rod Models for the Locomotion of Soft Robots — *Soft Robotics* 2019 (Paley/Majidi/O'Reilly).** Calibrated DER for caterpillar-style soft robots.

#### Position-Based Dynamics (PBD/XPBD) for ropes
**Liu, Su, Li, Ji, Yip et al. — "Differentiable Robotic Manipulation of Deformable Rope-like Objects Using Compliant Position-based Dynamics." arXiv 2202.09714, 2022.** XPBD-based differentiable simulator with parameter ID, demonstrated on Baxter and dVRK.

**Müller et al. — Position-Based Dynamics (original 2007).** Foundational reference for PBD.

**Macklin, Müller — XPBD (2016).** Extended PBD that decouples stiffness from iteration count.

#### Generic simulators usable for sim-to-real rope policies
- **MuJoCo 3 / MJX:** introduces *flex* deformables (1D segments = ropes, 2D = cloth, 3D = volumetric) and a native cable/composite model with linear stiffness; MJX provides JAX/XLA differentiable variant. Recently extended via **MuJoCo Warp** (~252×–475× speedups on Blackwell GPUs).
- **Newton (NVIDIA + Google DeepMind, 2025):** unified solver supporting MuJoCo + Vertex Block Descent (VBD) for cables/cloth/volumetric deformables and implicit MPM, with two-way coupling to rigid bodies. Best contemporary candidate for whip-policy sim-to-real.
- **Isaac Lab / Isaac Sim / PhysX 5:** GPU-native, photoreal, supports FEM cloth, PBD ropes/inflatables, two-way coupling. Real2Sim2Real (Lim et al.) used Isaac Gym for cable casting. NVIDIA's **Isaac Lab** technical report (arXiv 2511.04831) details current support.
- **DiffTaichi (Hu et al., ICLR 2020).** Differentiable programming for physical sim with rope/mass-spring examples; widely used as a back-end for differentiable rope policies.
- **Nimble Physics (Werling, Omens, Lee, Exarchos, Liu — ICRA 2021).** Differentiable DART fork; excellent for articulated rigid body but limited for ropes — typically combined with rope-specific simulators.
- **Brax, Warp, Tiny Differentiable Simulator, Dojo, DiffCoSim** — alternatives surveyed in Zhong, Han, Brikis, "Differentiable Physics Simulations with Contacts: Do They Have Correct Gradients?" (arXiv 2207.05060), an essential reference for choosing a simulator for whipping.
- **PlasticineLab, DaXBench, SoftMAC, DiffPD, DiffCloth** — soft-body / cloth-leaning differentiable simulators.
- **DiffCloud (Sundaresan, Antonova, Bohg).** Real2sim from point clouds via differentiable simulation+rendering; methodologically transferable to rope parameter ID.

## Recommendations

**If the goal is to build a robot system that whips a rope's tip to a 3D target:**

1. **Start by reproducing IRP** (Chi et al., RSS 2022) as the baseline. The code is public on the Columbia CAIR group page, the task is exactly "rope tip to 3D goal with a UR5," and the iterative-residual scheme has been validated on multiple ropes. This sets your real-world performance floor and gives you the right action parameterization (low-dimensional swing parameters) to compare against.
2. **For simulation, choose Discrete Elastic Rods** integrated either into MuJoCo (Chen, Bretl, Pham 2023/2025 — DER-MuJoCo) or as DEFORM (differentiable DER, 2024). DER captures bending/twisting at high speed accurately, and the MuJoCo integration gives clean robot–rope coupling. PyElastica is the alternative if you prefer Python-native Cosserat. **Do not** rely solely on PBD/XPBD or MuJoCo's native composite cable for *whipping*; the DER and Cosserat formulations are the only ones validated against high-curvature, high-speed regimes that are physically relevant to whips (per Goriely & McMillen, the loop dynamics matter).
3. **Use a structured action representation, not raw joint trajectories.** Empirical evidence across IRP, Real2Sim2Real, Robots-of-the-Lost-Arc, IPA, and DA-MMP all converges on parameterized motions (apex points, motion-manifold latents, or DMP/MMP trajectory parameters) being more sample-efficient, more transferable, and easier to refine on real hardware than full RL over joints. Lan et al. 2025 (DIDP) is the only counter-example in this list; treat it as a research direction rather than a default.
4. **Build in online adaptation rather than expecting one-shot zero-shot.** Both IRP's iterative residual and Wiggle-and-Go's wiggle-then-act show that physical rope diversity (mass, stiffness, knots, surface aging) makes pure open-loop sim-to-real fragile. Plan for at minimum a system-ID probe followed by 1–10 real refinement iterations.
5. **Borrow the "residual physics" idea from TossingBot** — overlay a learned residual on a physics-based controller (e.g., a Cosserat-rod-derived feed-forward trajectory). This combines best of analytic generalization and data-driven correction and was the architecture that won RSS 2019 Best Systems for the analogous rigid-throw problem.

**Decision thresholds that would change the recommendation:**

- **If pure sim-to-real precision <5 cm tip error is required at high speed:** abandon any simulator that uses linear-stiffness rope models (MuJoCo native composite, basic mass-spring); stay with DER or Cosserat.
- **If your hardware allows ≥1 kHz visual feedback:** the Yamakawa/Ishikawa-style high-speed visual feedback approach becomes competitive without learning, and you should benchmark your learned policy against it.
- **If your task lacks repeatability (one-shot only):** abandon iterative residual; move to one-shot policies like IPA (Wang & Qureshi 2025), DA-MMP, or motion-manifold methods.
- **If targets span 3D with complex obstacles:** Robots-of-the-Lost-Arc's 3D apex-point representation is the most directly applicable parameterization.
- **If the rope has heterogeneous mass (e.g., a payload on the tip):** IPA (2025) is currently the only paper in this literature explicitly tackling heterogeneous soft-tool dynamics; default to its formulation.

## Caveats

- **Several papers cited above are 2025–2026 arXiv preprints** (Bai et al. SIMPAR 2025; Lan et al. DIDP arXiv 2505.17434; SPiD arXiv 2602.03623; SoMA arXiv 2602.02402; Flying Knots arXiv 2602.21302; Prior-Reinforce arXiv 2505.21916; Implicit Physics-aware Policy arXiv 2502.05696). Performance numbers are from authors' own evaluations; peer-reviewed venue placement and independent reproduction are still in progress.
- **"Wiggle and Go!"** is referenced in citation lists and a 2024 IJRR follow-up to IRP, but its independent paper venue and authorship details I could not confirm from the searches; treat the description above as based on cited summary rather than a verified primary read of the paper.
- **The term "goal-conditioned dynamic rope tip targeting" is small enough as a literal task** that one should expect the next 12–24 months to produce more direct entries; the 2025 surge (Bai, Lan, Wang & Qureshi, SPiD, DA-MMP, DMMP) suggests the field is consolidating around diffusion policies and motion-manifold latents as the dominant action representations and DER/Cosserat as the dominant simulators.
- **Bullwhip physics (Goriely/McMillen) and human-motor-control (Sternad/Hogan)** are *not* robotics papers and provide constraints/inspiration rather than methods. None of the surveyed robot-learning papers reproduces a true supersonic loop in simulation; all use sub-supersonic regimes with moderate tip speeds.
- **The Yamakawa/Ishikawa work** is largely Japanese-language for some venues and uses simplified algebraic deformation approximations; its high success rates rely on 1 kHz vision and a specialized 4-DOF Barrett high-speed arm, conditions not available on most contemporary research platforms. Adapting their approach to standard 7-DOF arms (Franka, UR5) would require reformulation.
- **"FlingBot" and "DextAIRity" are cloth, not rope** — they are included as methodological analogs because their pick-stretch-fling primitive and self-supervised framework directly inspired rope-flicking work (and were co-authored by the same Columbia group as IRP), but they do not condition on a 3D point target.
- **Several throwing analogs** (Hutter group whole-body, badminton, goalkeeper, table tennis) target *moving* objects or use *whole-body* motion; transferring their RL-with-PPO-in-Isaac-Gym recipes to a deformable rope is non-trivial and has not yet been published end-to-end.
- **"Yamakawa et al. 2012 high-speed knotting"** is sometimes cited as 2010 (IROS 2010) and sometimes 2013 (IJARS 2013); they are different papers in the same line. The IJARS 2013 paper is the most complete reference.
