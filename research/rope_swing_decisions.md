# Rope-Swing Research — Decisions Log

## 2026-06-16 — Control regime: open-loop is core; closed-loop wind-up deferred
**Decision.** The core research focus remains **RL-training a zero-shot, open-loop swing** (target → a single open-loop joint trajectory; no per-target adaptation; no intra-swing feedback).

**Considered but deferred (NOT core).** A closed-loop variant limited to the slow **wind-up / limit-cycle phase** — feedback to stabilize the pre-strike initial condition, then commit to an open-loop strike ("option #3"). Recorded as a possible future fallback only.

**Rationale.**
1. Open-loop zero-shot is the actual research contribution; closed-loop reduces it to re-applied MPC (less novel).
2. A whip's terminal phase is near-uncontrollable by the handle *regardless of hardware* (wave-propagation delay L/c), so intra-strike feedback would help least exactly where targeting is decided. Feedback's value is front-loaded onto the slow wind-up — which is what option #3 captures.
3. Likely not achievable with current hardware anyway.

**Implication.** Keep feedback as a future *dial on the wind-up only*; do not architect the pipeline around closed-loop correction. Frame "RL-train a zero-shot swing" as **amortized trajectory optimization over a compact action**.

See: `rope_swing_research_handover.md`, `rope_swing_field_report.md`.

## 2026-07-24 — Independent re-derivation of the sim campaign; direction first-class
**Decisions.** (1) Plans are re-derived from task physics + primary literature, not inherited from wiki conclusions (user instruction). (2) **Direction is a first-class goal component from day 1** (reversing a brief position-first staging): goal space is 5D (3 position + 2 direction), 4D canonical after base-azimuth equivariance — a qualitatively different regime from all prior rope work (2–3D goals). (3) Per-step RL and PETS/forward-model machinery are **rejected for the sim phase** (the sim is its own perfect model; DaXBench: PPO 0.25 vs 0.83 on WhipRope). (4) Operating regime is **smooth swings** (curvature radius ~2 m, per user): direction sensitivity to timing jitter is benign (~1–3°); the open direction question is **coverage** (which arrival directions are reachable per position under smoothness), not precision. Campaign plan: `rope_swing_sim_experiment_plan.md`.

## 2026-07-25 — Empirical anchor: PPO works in-plane, fails in 3D; base method chosen
**Fact.** An in-plane, position-only PPO experiment works; scaling it to 3D fails. Diagnosis: exploration/sparse-supervision failure (near-hits become rare in 3D; reward slope vanishes under policy noise), consistent with external evidence — not a capacity problem.
**Decision.** Base method = **per-timestep hindsight relabeling + conditional flow matching over smooth basis parameters + sim-verified best-of-N selection**. Rationale: hindsight removes exploration from the problem entirely (every rollout supervises its achieved goal); a generative head handles the one-to-many inverse; sim verification is legitimate offline planning under the brief. **MMP++ is NOT the base**: its smoothness lives in its curve-parameter layer (which we share), not its latent; manifold/AE methods are deferred to a controlled shootout arm once there is data worth compressing. Arm-command smoothness is guaranteed by construction (smooth basis); rope-tip smoothness is enforced by filter + cost + deployment-time verification (no architecture can guarantee it).

## 2026-07-29 — Task box pre-registered; simulator identified; equivariance retracted; velocity cap set
**Simulator of record identified.** `DeformX/Cosserat-Rod-Sim-CUDA` (private, DeformX org): Stable Cosserat Rods solver batched for Isaac Lab; wall-mounted UR5 + 0.8 m tube + 1.0 m rope; **60 Hz control** (100 substeps/frame — the brief's "10,000 ropes at 100 Hz" is corrected: typical 512–2048 envs, ~153k env-steps/s @2048 on RTX 4090 → 10⁶ rollouts ≈ 20 min); rope-node state velocities exposed zero-copy; `--export` provides the open-loop joint-target interface.
**Decision — task box pre-registered (locked before any sweep), plan §6.6.** Adopted from the repo's own goal spec with two amendments: (1) dome trimmed to **1.5–2.4 m** (outer band at dynamic-reach limit; note the rationale asymmetry per user clarification — only the outer rim is a kinematic bound; the inner rim and axis-projection floor are task-design choices, since the rope can swing inward past them: the box governs evaluation claims only, data is never filtered to it, and the atlas logs inside-rim reachability as a diagnostic); (2) committed stratified reporting — grid {10,5,2 cm}×{30,15,7.5°}×speed {0.3,1,3 m/s}, direction bands vs radial (0–45/45–90/90–135/135–180°), atlas reachable fraction as headline, success-vs-budget curve. Direction sampling stays uniform-S² (reachability handled by stratification, never by post-hoc set shrinking). Primary endpoint: 5 cm ∧ 30° ∧ ≥0.3 m/s, swept-segment detection.
**Decision — joint-velocity cap is a pre-registered system parameter (user).** Primary **0.5·π ≈ 1.57 rad/s** (half UR5 limit — tracking-faithful, smooth-regime), secondary **0.8·π ≈ 2.51 rad/s** (repo's 20%-derate). Coverage-vs-cap reported as a curve; the task box does not move with the cap; full limit unused pending the real-arm tracking check.
**Retraction.** The azimuth-equivariance reduction (5D→4D canonical goal space) is **retracted**: the rig is wall-mounted (base joint axis horizontal), so base rotation is not gravity-equivariant. At most a left-right mirror augmentation survives, pending rig-symmetry validation. DeformX's "planar swings + base rotation" argument does not transfer to this mount.
**Repo nit to resolve.** README says "0.8 m tube" (coupling) vs "tube 0.65" (reach breakdown) — pin down, it defines the dome's outer rim.

DeformX-CMU needed no ingest — it was already in the wiki as `papers/deformx-versatile-co-simulation-framework-deformable`, and it *is* the 6.6 cm paper.

**Both novelty deltas VERIFIED against primary text (previously secondhand).**
(1) *Arrival direction* — checked against the DLO-Lab source: it is not merely position-only, it is **not goal-conditioned at all** (all 10 tasks bake a fixed goal into a hand-written reward; Slingshot rewards the cube's y-coordinate along a hard-coded axis). The field's newest DLO benchmark still treats goals as scene constants, which widens the gap.
(2) *Per-timestep relabeling* — confirmed from DA-MMP's text: conditioning is on a **single executed landing point per trajectory** via classifier-free guidance, 60 throws → 60 training pairs. **Sharpen the claim's wording:** DA-MMP does use hindsight relabeling (achieved outcome, not commanded target); our delta is **label multiplicity**, not relabeling per se. The reason they cannot do better is *task structure* — a released ring lands once, a rope tip is continuously in goal space — which is a stronger defense than an oversight claim.

**Corrections to the 2026-07-28 field-scan entry above (secondhand errors).**

2. **DA-MMP is not part of the Lee/Park MMP line** — it is Chi Chu & Huazhe Xu (Shanghai Qi Zhi / Tsinghua IIIS), an independent group building on MMP++.
3. **DMMP's paper is available**, contrary to the "coming soon" on diffmmp.github.io — it is on arXiv at 2410.12193 (that page's *code* link remains unreleased). MMFP's two apparent titles are **one paper**: 2407.19681 was retitled at v3 for RA-L 2025; the ledger's "MMFP RA-L 2025" was correct.

**Decision — plan changes adopted from the ingest (v3.3 candidates, not yet written into the plan).**
- **Stage B arm B4 is respecified.** Its original form (decoder f(z,g), z ∈ ℝ²⁻³, shared latent prior) is the TC-VAE/MMP architecture that MMFP shows collapsing (9.3%/15.3% joint accuracy) exactly when conditioning shrinks the support — which is our own H4. Run B4 in **MMFP form** (unconditional manifold + conditional latent flow) and **sweep latent dim m ∈ {2,4,8,16,32}**; reference implementations use m = 3 / 32 / 64, so 2–3 was an order of magnitude low.
- **New arm B3-TMO.** Trajectory Manifold Optimization (from DMMP) is architecture-agnostic and drops onto our CFM head without a manifold; it is where all of DMMP's gain came from (17.5% → 95.8%) and it holds seen→unseen generalization at 95.8 → 94.1 where the untuned baseline collapses 77.4 → 15.0. Arm-side constraints only — the rope tip is not closed-form differentiable in w.
- **New Stage-A step A1.5: double-execution repeatability filter.** Execute each swept trajectory twice, keep only if outcomes agree, *before* building the relabeled pool (DA-MMP does this for ring-tossing). A chaotic swing that passes a goal once teaches the amortizer a lie, and downstream verification cannot remove it from the training distribution.
- **Stage C gains a concrete second verifier.** DLO-Lab (Apache-2.0, released) accepts externally supplied open-loop joint-position sequences via `eval_traj(..., qpos=...)` with IK bypassed, and already randomizes mass/bending/stretching stiffness on reset. ~25× slower than the primary stack — fine for ranking N = 64, unusable as a data engine. **Caveat: it models no aerodynamic drag**, the same blind spot as our own simulator, so it cannot arbitrate the drag term.
- **Stage D D5 gate tightened.** DLO-Lab's own benchmark has analytic-gradient descent at the do-nothing floor (0% on all three tool-use tasks) vs CMA-ES 93% on Slingshot, because ∂r/∂a ≡ 0 before contact. Our task has no pre-contact dead zone, but the closest-approach soft-min direction term reintroduces ruggedness. D5 is scoped to polishing a CMA-ES-selected candidate inside a smooth basin, and must beat CMA-ES refinement of the same candidate to enter.

**Claim to defend, now with an external counterexample.** DA-MMP's autoencoder ablation found flow matching over *raw* trajectory parameters produced "irregular and oscillatory", "rarely executable" joint profiles — i.e. its manifold was load-bearing for **executability**. Our escape is that the decoder enforces smoothness and joint/velocity/acceleration limits by construction for every parameter vector, including out-of-distribution ones. That escape must be stated explicitly in any writeup; if the decoder property ever weakens, the autoencoder stage returns.
