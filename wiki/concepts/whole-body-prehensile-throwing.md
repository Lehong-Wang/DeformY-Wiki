## Definition

**Whole-body prehensile throwing** is the task of grasping an object with a robot's gripper and releasing it mid-motion to land on a specified target, where the throwing motion is generated by *coordinating the entire body* of a legged mobile manipulator — base posture, leg torque, torso lean, and arm joints — rather than only the arm. The "prehensile" qualifier distinguishes it from non-prehensile push/strike throwing (where the object is contacted but not grasped); the "whole-body" qualifier distinguishes it from fixed-base or arm-only throwing.

The canonical formulation casts the task as **time-dependent end-effector (EE) state tracking** on the *goal manifold of object flight*: any release state $(\bm{p}_d, \bm{v}_d)$ whose subsequent ballistic trajectory lands inside the target set is acceptable, so the controller does not need to track a single point but a manifold of equivalent terminal configurations.

## Intuition

Three forces motivate whole-body over arm-only throwing:

1. **Reach extension via base motion**: a stationary arm cannot release at high horizontal velocity without exotic joint configurations; coordinating base lean, leg push, and arm swing extends the kinetic envelope.
2. **Angular impulse contribution**: in this paper's measurement, the legged base of an ANYmal-D contributes ~53% more angular impulse to the throw than the arm alone running the same joint trajectory, with 51.7 N·m·s and 46.3 J of work passed through to the arm prior to release.
3. **Posture-dependent release windows**: orienting the torso and base lets the gripper aim release velocity at directions otherwise unreachable, and lets the gripper opening face away from the throw direction to avoid finger occlusion.

The complexity cost is that an 18-joint legged manipulator is harder to model and control than a 6-DOF arm, especially for highly dynamic motions where the base ground-contact dynamics matter.

## Formal notation

Goal manifold formulation (Pekarovskiy 2013): given a target landing set $\mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ over $(r, z, \dot{r}, \dot{z})$ and a flight flowmap $\Phi$, the **goal manifold** is

$$
\mathcal{M} = \{ (\bm{p}_d, \bm{v}_d) \mid \exists \xi \in \mathcal{X},\ \Phi(\bm{p}_d, \bm{v}_d) = \xi \}
$$

The whole-body controller maps environment+target observations $o_t$ through joint commands $q_t$ to terminal EE state $\bm{x}_T(o, q) \in \mathcal{M}$. Equivalently: track a time-varying EE state reference $(\bm{p}_d^*(t), \bm{v}_d^*(t))$ over $t \in [0, T_{throw}]$ such that $T_{throw}$ falls inside the gripper's release window.

## Variants

- **Open-loop whole-body throw** (Munn et al. 2024; Ha et al. 2024): trains a single end-to-end policy for the throw motion; reports qualitative results without quantified hardware accuracy.
- **Goal-manifold whole-body throw** (this paper): explicit reference tracking on $\mathcal{M}$ + high-frequency residual + closed-loop pullback tube on the release window. Quantified accuracy on hardware (0.276 m mean landing error at 6 m).
- **Excavator-scale dynamic throw** (Werner et al. 2024): RL on a 12-ton multipurpose excavator; the same loco-manipulation principle at industrial scale.
- **Fixed-base prehensile throw** (TossingBot, Zeng et al. 2020; Liu and Billard 2024): the antecedent — same task formulation without the legged base contribution.
- **Non-prehensile whole-body push-throw**: extension to objects that cannot be grasped (cylindrical rolls, sliders); not yet demonstrated quantitatively.

## Comparison

- vs. **fixed-base arm throwing** (TossingBot, Werner et al.): whole-body adds reach and angular impulse but at the cost of much harder dynamics modeling and a large sim-to-real gap.
- vs. **non-prehensile dynamic manipulation** (Mason 1993; Lynch 1999): non-prehensile throws skip release-uncertainty entirely but cannot handle irregular objects.
- vs. **whole-body locomotion on rough terrain** (e.g. Portela 2024): the loco-manipulation problem inherits whole-body locomotion controllers but adds the terminal accuracy constraint.

## When to use

- Target is beyond arm reach (delivery, throwing into elevated bins, dynamic interactions).
- Object is irregular (cannot be reliably push-thrown).
- The robot is a legged mobile manipulator with fast-rate state estimation.
- The throw must be repeatable (the legged base contribution shows up only at high commanded velocities).

Skip when a 6-DOF arm-only throw suffices, when the platform cannot deliver a 400 Hz state estimate, or when the target accuracy budget is loose enough to tolerate end-to-end RL without explicit release-time robustness.

## Known limitations

- The whole-body advantage is regime-specific: at low commanded velocities the base stays largely stationary; the full-body coordination only emerges at high velocities (≥6 m/s in this paper).
- The 18-joint policy training cost is substantial (4500 PPO iterations, ~2500 sim-hours just for the nominal policy).
- Sim-to-real gap on the residual policy; a piece of the simulator-side performance is not realized on hardware.
- Aerodynamics, spin, and object inertia are typically neglected; deformable or asymmetric objects break those assumptions.

## Open problems

- Whole-body prehensile *whipping* of soft / deformable objects: how does the same architecture generalize when the "object" is a flexible cable or rope?
- Closed-loop visuomotor whole-body throwing: replace AprilTag target localization with onboard vision and propagate target uncertainty through the BRT/pullback stack.
- Mass / inertia adaptation during the preparation phase, so the controller can throw heavier or asymmetric loads without retraining.
- Provable safety guarantees on a 58 kg legged manipulator throwing at 10 m/s — collision avoidance with humans, fail-safe behaviors, fall recovery from a high-velocity throw.

## Key papers

- [[learning-accurate-whole-body-throwing-high]] — introduces goal-manifold whole-body throwing on ANYmal-D + DynaArm with quantified hardware accuracy (0.276 m mean landing error at 6 m); first reported instance of prehensile whole-body throwing with quantified accuracy on hardware.

## My understanding

Whole-body prehensile throwing is the canonical test bed for any control stack that has to combine *learning* (for the slow open-loop motion) with *model-based optimization* (for the fast closed-loop terminal phase). The IROS 2025 paper offers the cleanest separation of concerns to date. For DeformY-style dynamic manipulation, the relevant transfer is whether the *whole-body* premise still buys angular impulse when the payload is a flexible cable rather than a rigid object. If yes, the legged base is an under-explored amplifier for dynamic deformable manipulation.
