## Problem setting

A task objective $J(q(\cdot),\eta;\tau)$ and a differentiable constraint vector
$C(q,\dot q,\ddot q,\dddot q)\le 0$ are **given** — no demonstrations. For every task
parameter $\tau$ in a continuous space $\mathcal{T}$, produce multiple feasible motions
$(q(t),\eta)$ in **sub-second** time, where $\eta$ is an auxiliary discrete-event variable
(release time, contact instant). The regime that makes this hard: the task can only be
achieved by saturating the constraints, so solutions sit on the constraint boundary and
per-query trajectory optimization takes 10–3000 s and is initialization-sensitive.

Distinguishing feature versus the rest of the [[motion-manifold-primitives]] family: the
"demonstrations" are self-generated by an offline optimizer, and constraint satisfaction —
not imitation fidelity — is the objective.

## Mechanism

Four stages; the first is a data factory, the middle two are the model, the fourth is the
contribution.

1. **Offline trajectory optimization as data source.** Trajectories are parameterized by a
   fixed boundary-pinned smooth basis
   $q(t)=q_0+(q_T-q_0)(3-2s)s^2+s^2(s-1)^2\Phi(s)w$, $s=t/T$, with normalized Gaussian bases
   ($B=20$), enforcing $q(0)=q_0,\ q(T)=q_T,\ \dot q(0)=\dot q(T)=0$. Adam with a ReLU
   constraint penalty, many random $(q_0,q_T,w)$ seeds per $\tau$, yields a diverse solution
   pool.
2. **Differentiable Motion Manifold (DMM).** Encoder $g_\alpha((q_1,\dots,q_L),\eta)=z$;
   decoder $f_\beta(z,t)=(\hat q_\beta(z,t),\eta_\beta(z))$ — the decoder takes *time as an
   input*, so its output is analytic in $t$ and $\dot{\hat q},\ddot{\hat q},\dddot{\hat q}$
   come from autodiff. The decoder body is a DeepONet-style **linear basis-function** model
   $\hat q_\beta(z,t)=\sum_{b=1}^{N_b}\psi^b_\beta(z)\,\theta^b_\beta(t)$: learned time bases
   $\theta^b(t)$ scaled by latent-dependent coefficients $\psi^b(z)$. Time derivatives never
   touch $\psi$, and a fixed $z$ compresses to the $N_b$-vector $\psi(z)$. Reconstruction loss
   is time-weighted by $c(t)$, concentrated near the event time $\eta$.
3. **Task-conditioned latent flow (→ DMMFP).** $p_\gamma(z|\tau)$ as the pushforward of a
   Gaussian through a neural velocity field $dz/ds=v_\gamma(s,\tau,z)$, trained by flow
   matching, sampled by forward-Euler ODE integration. Inherited from MMFP.
4. **Trajectory Manifold Optimization (TMO).** Freeze $\alpha,\gamma$; fine-tune the decoder
   $\beta$ on
   $w_{\rm recon}\mathcal{L}_{\rm recon}+\mathbb{E}_{t\sim U[0,T],\tau\sim U(\mathcal{T}),z\sim p_\gamma(\cdot|\tau)}[J+W^\top\mathrm{ReLU}(C)^2]$.
   Uniform sampling of $\tau$ over the *continuum* — not the finite grid the optimizer visited —
   is what produces generalization to unseen tasks. See
   [[trajectory-manifold-optimization]].

**Deployment adds a fifth, unlearned stage: rejection sampling (RS).** Generate $N$ latents in
parallel, decode, run the full constraint check on all $N$, discard violators. In the reference
implementation this is not optional — it is what takes success from 95.8% to 100%.

The reusable design claim: *put diversity in a low-dimensional latent with a conditional flow,
put continuous-time structure in a decoder that eats $t$, put the specification into a
fine-tuning loss, and keep a verifier at deployment anyway.*

## Procedure

**Offline:**
1. Choose a finite grid $\mathcal{T}_s\subset\mathcal{T}$; solve the constrained trajectory
   optimization many times per grid point with randomized initialization; discard failures.
2. Train $(g_\alpha,f_\beta)$ on the surviving trajectories with the $c(t)$-weighted
   reconstruction loss plus $\|\eta_\beta(z)-\eta\|^2$.
3. Encode the dataset to $\{(\tau_i,\{z_{ij}\})\}$; train $v_\gamma$ by flow matching.
4. TMO: freeze $\alpha,\gamma$, fine-tune $\beta$ on the task + constraint-penalty loss with
   $\tau\sim U(\mathcal{T})$.

**Online (per query $\tau$):**
1. Sample $N$ latents from $p_\gamma(z|\tau)$ by integrating the latent ODE; decode all in a
   batch.
2. Evaluate all constraints on all $N$ trajectories at $L$ time points; keep the feasible set.
3. Select — by task error, or (for reactive replanning) by
   $(i^*,t^*)=\arg\min_{i,t}\|q_c-q_i(t)\|$ s.t. $t<\eta_i$, i.e. the candidate closest to the
   *current* configuration.
4. If replanning mid-motion, splice a transition trajectory from $(q_c,\dot q_c)$ to
   $(q_{i^*}(t^*),\dot q_{i^*}(t^*))$ using a boundary-velocity-matching version of the same
   curve model; random Gaussian init of its $w$ usually yields a feasible one immediately.

Representative settings (7-DoF arm, throwing): latent dim $m=32$; $N_b=100$ decoder bases;
$B=20$ optimizer bases; 4–6 layer, 1024-wide GELU MLPs; $T=5$ s; Euler step $ds=0.1$;
$c(t)=\exp(-4(t-\eta)^2)$; $N=100$ samples per query; 45 constraint checks per state tuple.

## Assumptions

- The objective and the full constraint stack (FK, inverse dynamics, collision margins) are
  implemented **differentiably** — the paper's are all in PyTorch.
- An offline optimizer exists that can solve the problem at all, however slowly, and can be
  seeded to produce *diverse* solutions; diversity of the pool bounds diversity of the manifold.
- Task-parameter symmetries have been factored out by hand where possible (the reference case
  fixes target azimuth to zero and rotates joint 1 at deployment).
- The event variable $\eta$ is a function of the latent alone, i.e. the timing is a property of
  the motion rather than of the task parameter.
- Open-loop execution of the generated trajectory is acceptable between replans; no tracking
  controller is modelled.

## Limitations

- **The differentiable-manifold architecture alone underperforms the discrete-time baseline it
  replaces** (17.5% vs 77.4% success on seen task parameters, pre-TMO). All the gain is in stage 4.
- **Soft constraints only.** TMO leaves joint-velocity violations at 7–20%; a hard guarantee
  requires the rejection-sampling verifier, whose 0.2 s dominates the 0.012 s of generation.
- **No latent-dimension or architecture ablation.** $m=32$ is used unexamined on a task family
  with 2 effective conditioning dimensions; no isometric/geometric latent regularization despite
  the same author's [[mmp-parametric-curve-motion-manifold-primitives]] showing latent distortion
  breaks density fitting.
- **The manifold's necessity is untested**: no baseline fine-tunes a conditional generator over
  the *fixed* curve parameters $w$ of stage 1 with the same TMO loss, which the analytic
  differentiability of $q(t;w)$ would allow.
- **Frozen encoder / moved decoder** are no longer mutually inverse after TMO; latent semantics
  are not maintained.
- **Simulation only**; no hardware, no tracking control, and the reference experiment doubles the
  robot's end-effector velocity limits to make the task feasible.
- **No public code or camera-ready** as of 2026-07-30 (`diffmmp.github.io` — "Coming soon").

## Tradeoff profile

- **Plan-time compute**: 0.012 s to generate 100 trajectories on an RTX 3090; +0.215 s if the
  feasibility verifier runs. Against 10–3000 s for SLSQP/COBYLA/Adam trajectory optimization on
  the same problem — 3 to 5 orders of magnitude, and 100 diverse solutions instead of one.
- **Task success**: 95.8% (seen) / 94.1% (unseen) at 4 cm tolerance without verification;
  100% / 100% with it. Trajectory optimization reaches 97% / 100% but at ≥10 s and 33–97%
  convergence depending on solver.
- **Constraint satisfaction**: 93–100% per constraint family post-TMO, versus 0.0% on
  velocity/acceleration/jerk/torque for discrete-time MMP and MMFP fitted to the same data.
- **Generalization to unseen task parameters**: the standout number — MMFP falls 77.4 → 15.0,
  DMMFP+TMO holds 95.8 → 94.1. Attributable to $\tau\sim U(\mathcal{T})$ in TMO, not to the
  architecture.
- **Training cost**: four stages including a fine-tuning stage that evaluates the whole
  constraint stack at sampled $(t,\tau,z)$ each step; substantially heavier than plain MMP
  fitting.
- **Data cost**: ~29% yield from the offline optimizer (3,523 of 12,000 attempts in the
  reference run), and yield degrades as the task gets harder.
- **Guarantees**: none from the model; feasibility comes only from the deployment-time verifier.
